A social media user posts a photo of someone else without their permission, along with personal information about that person. This action potentially violates several laws. Here are some explanations:

1. **Copyright Law**: The user may have violated the copyright law by sharing the photo without obtaining the consent of the owner or photographer. The person in the photo may have intellectual property rights over their image.
2. **Right to Privacy** (Article 28(2) of the Indonesian Constitution): By sharing personal information and a private photo without permission, the user has invaded the privacy of the individual featured in the photo. This violation can be considered a form of harassment or stalking, which is illegal.
3. **Data Protection Law**: In Indonesia, the Personal Data Protection Regulation (Peraturan Pelindung Data Pribadi) governs the handling and sharing of personal data. Sharing someone's personal information without consent may violate this regulation, as it involves processing personal data without the individual's knowledge or permission.
4. **Defamation Law** (Article 390 of the Indonesian Criminal Code): If the user makes false accusations about the person in the photo, they may be liable for defamation. This can lead to a criminal complaint and legal consequences.

To summarize, the social media user has potentially violated several laws by sharing a photo without permission and including personal information:

* Copyright law (intellectual property rights)
* Right to Privacy (invasion of privacy)
* Data Protection Law (processing personal data without consent)
* Defamation Law (making false accusations)

It is essential for users to respect the privacy and intellectual property rights of others when using social media.
